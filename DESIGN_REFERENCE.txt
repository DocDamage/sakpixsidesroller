THE ERROR TO CORRECT
We treated reachable platforms, climb counts, supported props and passing route
tests as evidence of good level design. Those establish basic function. They do
not establish enjoyable decisions, orientation, pacing, composition or atmosphere.
Adding detail to a weak room section preserves the weak section.

OBSERVATIONS TO APPLY, WITH EXAMPLES TO REOPEN
1. Shape the whole room, including the solids around the playable void.
   00:03-00:07 Moss Grotto: thick rock masses, ceiling overhangs, cavities,
   narrow throats and openings. The floor is only one boundary of the design.
   In the dense opening window, a broad ground fight leads into a rising series
   of landings, enemies and spike-edged ceilings, then a lower crossing.
   The next landing, threat and recovery space give each platform a purpose.
   Floating platforms can be intentional; banning them misses the lesson.

2. Verticality needs actions and changing constraints.
   03:14:30-03:15:18 Shellwood: a short approach opens into a winding shaft.
   Wall contours vary the space; alternating wall shelves provide pauses while
   enemies and later thorn surfaces change which side is safe to approach.
   The player stops, fights, repositions and climbs. More ladder length would
   not reproduce those decisions. Do not copy later-game hazard density into
   our introductory rooms without teaching the required movement first.

3. Architecture can make a simple climb coherent.
   07:17:00-07:17:48 Grand Gate: an open lit approach leads through a threshold
   into a tall hall. Balconies attach to columns; a few hang from chains.
   Repeated tall windows and darker columns establish scale and readable layers.
   The climb itself repeats simple jumps, including retries. Variety does not
   require a different mechanic every few seconds. What matters here is a clear
   local rule, convincing architecture, readable landings and recovery.

4. Compose a sequence of different room roles.
   00:12-00:15 Bone Bottom: inhabited refuge with shelters, lamps and occupants
   clustered around common ground. 01:35-01:41: traversal/combat, quiet forge,
   then Lace's broad arena. 08:34-08:46: Songclave frontage and Cogwork Dancers.
   Alternate low passages, shafts, wide fights, overlooks, refuges and rewards.
   Intentional empty action space is valuable. Uniform visual density is not.

5. Fit an encounter to its space and the player's available movement.
   02:56-02:59 Moorwing: broad ground lane and tall airspace serve a flying boss.
   08:40-08:46 Cogwork Dancers: clear floor and overhead volume serve two mobile
   opponents. 11:12-11:17: wave combat uses a lower floor and central upper shelf.
   Choose an approach, a readable threat, an escape/recovery option and a reward.
   Enemy counts and stronger impact effects cannot fix poorly staged encounters.
   Exact telegraph, hit-stop and hitbox tuning still need live game verification.

6. Give progression a spatial reason to return.
   05:25-05:26 Great Gate: a recognizable gate makes the missing requirement
   meaningful. Revisited towns and landmarks recur throughout the walkthrough.
   10:31:20-10:32:08: low lava crossings lead to a framed Clawline reward, then
   the player returns through high anchors already visible along the approach.
   Present a goal before its key, and create a new useful action after a reward.
   Our physical boss return is necessary; compact recognizable loops and
   unfinished destinations should make using it worthwhile.

7. Build depth from distinct planes and plausible light.
   06:52-07:11 Underworks: fixtures, vents, masonry and diagonal shafts of light
   organize openings and landings. Grand Gate's windows light the room behind
   darker playable architecture. In late arenas, restrained backgrounds leave
   room for large temporary combat effects. Darkness alone does not create mood.
   Separate collision silhouettes, enemies, architecture and distance by value,
   color and overlap. Reserve foreground occlusion for deliberate framing.

8. Props should explain a place or activity.
   NPC workspaces, transport stations, shrines and inhabited shelters have
   functional clusters. Match asset perspective, ground contact and scale.
   Keep landing lips, combat recovery lanes and interactables readable. Blank
   space is not an instruction to scatter another urn, candle or campfire.

9. Change the terrain grammar between locations.
   Deep Docks (01:07-01:18) combines cave forms with working loading machinery.
   Cogwork Core (08:49-09:08) uses gears as room shapes and movement constraints.
   Mount Fay (11:20-11:36) uses snow banks, narrow rock throats and angled ice.
   Transitions should share materials, forms and activities before the new area's
   vocabulary takes over. A palette change or background swap is insufficient.

WATER AND AUDIO: KEEP EVIDENCE SEPARATE
Water imagery near 03:39-03:47 shows bounded water, banks and a bridge approach;
it does not establish swimming, currents or controllable water levels. Those are
our user's requested mechanics. Author banks, depth, inlet/outlet and visible
route choices first; then make surface motion, contact effects and underwater
readability support them. No SFX or OST conclusions were drawn from this silent
visual copy. The requested Haunted Manor OST and local SFX kit remain the audio
direction; listen to assets and live mixes when working on sound.

HOW TO USE THIS REFERENCE IN FUTURE WORK
Read STAGE1_DESIGN_AUDIT.txt, choose one room sequence and reopen its relevant
reference window before editing. Use atlas-index.json to find other passages.
If a lesson becomes vague, re-examine the source sequence instead of inventing
what it showed. review_window.py can extract a new timestamped 2-fps sequence:
  python artifacts/reference-study/review_window.py --start 07:17:00 --seconds 48 --name hall-revisit
The full local video is artifacts/reference-study/silksong-full-360p.mp4.

Before decorating, specify the room's purpose, entrance view, player decision,
movement constraints, recovery, reward, exit and return behavior. Check the full
room section and normal gameplay camera. Then verify with enemies active, ordinary
keyboard and controller inputs, both travel directions and missed optional items.
Connectivity automation is useful regression coverage; visual review and live
play are also required. The user's 10-20 minute first-clear goal remains unproven
until appropriate first-time playtesting. This expert twelve-hour walkthrough
does not establish an appropriate duration or pixel width for our stage.
