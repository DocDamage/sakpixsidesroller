STAGE 1 - DESIGN AUDIT AFTER THE SILKSONG VISUAL STUDY
2026-10-30. Original design brief; implementation progress is recorded below.
Read DESIGN_REFERENCE.txt for evidence, scope and limitations.

EVIDENCE REVIEWED IN OUR BUILD
Existing captures: artifacts/district_quarter.png, district_bell_landing.png,
district_drowned.png, district_chapel_undercroft.png, district_return_map.png.
Layout declaration: scripts/district_layout.gd. WIDTH is 19200, with named room
rectangles and long connecting floor runs, including a 6620px return gallery.
These captures demonstrate composition at specific positions; they are not a new
full-stage live playtest. No new claim about collision or combat feel is made.

1. CROOKED QUARTER - MAKE THE HUB A READABLE PLACE
Observed: the captured view is dominated by a uniform brick wall. A ladder runs
from the street beyond the camera top; two dark arches and ritual information
compete without a strong architectural hierarchy. Top and bottom HUD bands also
reduce usable view and vertical anticipation.
Proposed sequence: enter from a lower, compressed lane into a small open court.
Frame the ritual in one recognizable building frontage, show the bell tower above
an offset roof opening, and expose the lower-route grille at a distinct corner.
Use deep door recesses, roof silhouettes and a glimpse through an arch to give
the block thickness. Group useful props around the ritual/NPC workspace. Leave
the junction and landing clear. Introduce the sealed destination before sending
the player for seals; do not rely only on the objective text.
Check: from each entrance, a player can identify the court, the next available
route and the destination to revisit. Review HUD/camera space alongside geometry.

2. BELL ROUTE - REPLACE THE OPEN SCAFFOLD SECTION WITH A TOWER JOURNEY
Observed: the landing capture shows thin timber platforms, long straight posts,
an open empty background and a ladder extending out of view. Grounding the beams
fixed asset placement, but did not give the route a convincing building section.
Proposed sequence: apothecary interior stair and roof exit -> short gutter crossing
with a visible destination -> broken tower wall opening -> offset inner bell shaft
with intermediate alcoves -> bell mechanism/reward -> a nearby roof shortcut back
to the court. Solid wall masses and ceiling breaks should define the route before
planks are placed. Reuse existing building/ruin/timber assets at consistent scale.
Use a few jumps with different approach conditions; let sheltered ledges provide
recovery and one meaningful encounter. Do not fill every shelf with an enemy.
Check: jumps fit measured movement, next landing is visible before commitment,
fall recovery is legible, and the reward produces a useful return connection.

3. DROWNED GALLERY - DESIGN A WATER ROOM BEFORE ADDING SHADER COMPLEXITY
Observed: water fills much of a rectangular brick chamber. At the captured depth,
the player, coins and far shapes have weak separation. The surface and wall repeat
across the view; the image gives little spatial explanation of the water system.
Proposed sequence: shallow entry shelf -> pump overview showing high/low states ->
divided cistern around a substantial central pier -> optional lower swim passage ->
dry upper landing and flood-control destination. Put the inlet, overflow and gate
where their function is visible. Show a reward/shortcut from the safe overview.
Tie any current to the inlet/outlet; retain safe eddies and a readable exit.
Use water caustics/reflections/wakes/contact SFX to describe this geometry, with
underwater contrast tuned for enemies and route edges, not just more highlights.
Check: high and low water states, both directions, missed pump, player recovery,
controller swimming, foreground occlusion, and enemy readability while moving.

4. CHAPEL AND UNDERCROFT - MAKE THE RETURN LEG UNDERSTANDABLE
Observed: the undercroft capture is a broad flat brick hallway with two candles
and small station text. The map has branches but still reads strongly as one
long axis, with a long lower transport link. Connection alone does not create a
compact, memorable loop or explain the return from the boss's camera position.
Proposed sequence: establish chapel/door silhouette before the encounter -> clear
boss floor with useful overhead space -> reward pause -> obvious opened return arch
and visible first descent landing -> vaulted station chamber -> useful return to
the known court. Shorten empty travel where possible; use distinct side chambers
and overlooked destinations to make return choices matter. A tram can serve the
world, but cannot substitute for all local circulation.
Keep the existing post-boss physical route and release of missed optional gates
functional while rebuilding. Never make a new decoration obstruct it.
Check: defeat the boss before optional pump/prison switches, leave the Moon Key
or side loot unfinished, return on foot/climbs and transport, re-enter the chapel,
resume from results, and continue forward. Use normal gameplay camera and inputs.

5. ENCOUNTERS AND PRESENTATION ACROSS THE SEQUENCE
Place enemies after choosing room actions. A shield guard can hold a broad lane;
a ranged enemy can expose a safe approach decision; a flying enemy needs useful
airspace and recovery landings. Confirm exact roles against actual enemy behavior.
Do not tune all encounters as the same group with different sprites. Verify sword
contact, visual root stability, reaction, impact audio and escape space in live
combat; footage sampling cannot prove the earlier swing/teleport issues resolved.
Use the source's contrast between quiet and pressured rooms. Do not add hazards,
props, VFX and enemies to every available gap.

ORDER OF WORK
Start with the court-to-bell-to-court sequence as a coherent playable slice.
Define room cross-sections, local loop and camera reveals; build and play the
geometry; stage encounters; then dress and light. Expand the same discipline to
the cistern and chapel. Preserve controller operation and backtracking throughout.
Compare new captures with old captures and the specific reference passages.
Do not call all twelve stages polished because the first slice improves.

IMPLEMENTATION PROGRESS - COURT / APOTHECARY / BELL LOOP, 2026-10-30
An initial playable pass now implements the court frontage, two offset apothecary
stairs, enclosed loft, exposed roof crossing, tower piers/ceiling, alternating
stone landings and a separate belfry reward space. Windows retain their source
arch/sill pixel scale while extending panes vertically. Source-aligned lighting
and restrained beams distinguish the warm apothecary from the cooler tower.
The reward winch unlocks a callable bell cage at x4915; its descending shaft is
clear of intermediate ledges. It provides a separate return path to street level.
The exploration HUD uses two corner panels rather than full-width top/bottom bars.
Stage 1 has 40 enemies, 55 surfaces and 118 coins after this pass. These counts
describe content, not design quality or a measured first-clear duration.

The route is checked from spawn through the reward and back to the court using
native keyboard/controller events, normal health and active enemies. Three guards
are defeated through actual combat on that test route. Full-stage connectivity
and the missed-switch boss return are separate regression checks. A canal ledge
was raised eight pixels to remove a snag against the adjoining return rail.
GPU captures are in artifacts/district_bell_*_new.png; bell-live-*.png are taken
from the continuous input run, without repositioning between those captures.

The original cistern and chapel design briefs remain outstanding. Continue visual
review of the redesigned loop; passing its input route is not a substitute for
first-time human playtesting or a claim that the campaign is fully polished.

HOUSE INTEGRATION CORRECTION - 2026-10-30
The user rejected the first facade pass as disconnected and insufficiently blended.
The opening cottages, apothecary and roof annex have now been rebuilt using the
matching House Inside/Outside kit at a consistent 3x source-pixel scale. Complete
exteriors, continuous foundations/cornices, cutaway interior infills and physical
wall skins replace the earlier collage of miniature houses and detached roof art.
The apothecary's hearth contains the relocated fire; furniture fits specific bays.
Interior lighting uses the actual world positions of the baked window fixtures.
Rain shelter samples the opaque roof pixels. Closed facades do not receive interior
floor shadows. Overview renders use tests/housing_visuals.gd; normal camera views
include artifacts/district_housing_interior.png and district_housing_street.png.
Native keyboard/controller bell-loop runs and crop/placement/weather checks pass.
This correction covers the opening block; other buildings still need review.

FERR2D PACKAGE ASSESSMENT
The supplied Unity package was inspected without importing its files. It contains
Unity-dependent terrain/path/editor scripts, shaders, textures and examples.
It cannot run directly in this Godot project. Suitable terrain textures can be
evaluated for reuse and its path/edge/fill workflow can inform a native Godot tool.
No Ferr2D plugin port or engine migration has been performed. Inventory and source
path are recorded in artifacts/reference-study/ferr2d-package-review.json.

FERR2D / FIXTURE / ATTACK CORRECTION PASS - 2026-10-30
The assessment above is now followed by a native Godot adaptation of the inspected
directional-edge/fill workflow. Fourteen foundations use a shared authored contour
for collision, visible fill and shadow casting, with the package's unchanged local
RockyFill and existing pixel-art surface courses. This is not a Unity plugin port.

Direct asset inspection found a scaffold/table embedded in the ladder source and
an unrelated textile merged below the extracted fireplace mantel. Climbs now use
48px clean rails at uniform scale. Interior furnishings have supported footprints;
the mantel is cropped to its actual opening/base and paired with a separate fire.
Interior panes exclude exterior shutters. Open streets/balconies use lantern posts;
wall torches have rear architecture. Underground rear walls close the prison's sky
gap. Village and legacy town outdoor candelabras are also replaced by grounded posts.

Fire sprites now separate a horizontal log bed from four animated flame frames.
Glow, real district point lights and smoke reference the same visible emitter.
Exterior fixtures illuminate closed facades on a separate mask, avoiding shadows
from interior floors painted across the plaster. Interior lights still illuminate
the cutaway normally. Source alignment is checked in normal camera renders.

The Orc attack sheet was reviewed cell by cell: 0/1 anticipate, 2/3 sweep, 4/5
recover. Damage/swish/trail begin in the sweep; interrupted attacks lose their
attached effects. Anticipation/recovery no longer cause body-contact damage.
Ranged casts wait for the visible release phase, with launch/contact/terrain cues.
Twenty-eight derived attack sounds have local SFX provenance and remain excluded
from Git alongside all other media. Only source and text manifests are published.

Verification includes the twelve targeted fixture/attack checks; live normal-damage
keyboard/controller bell-loop replays with active enemies; both post-boss return
inputs with missed optional switches; all twelve controller guardian fights;
placement, crop, weather and renderer checks. Normal-camera renders were inspected
for the loft, hearth, street, prison, active blade sweep and village lanterns.
This establishes the correction's function and sampled presentation. It does not
complete the cistern/return-gallery redesign or the other eleven stage reviews.
